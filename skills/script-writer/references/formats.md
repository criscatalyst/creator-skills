# Formats and beat maps

The format decides the structure. Pick one, plan its beats, then write lines to fill the beats.

## The attention rules every format obeys

1. **One open loop, closed at the end.** The hook opens a question. The best answer, number or reveal arrives in the last 20% of the video. Once the loop closes, people leave: so close it right before the CTA.
2. **Re-hook every 6-8 seconds.** A new micro-question, a "but", a number that moves, a surprise on screen. Short-form viewers decide again every few seconds whether to stay.
3. **At least one pattern interrupt.** Something that breaks the rhythm: a hard cut, music stopping, a blunt judgment ("that's garbage"), a switch from face to screen, a reveal ("that wasn't me talking").
4. **Proof on screen, not in words.** Every claim that matters is *shown*: the output, the before/after, the dashboard, the price tag. The voice carries the rhythm, the screen carries the proof.
5. **Stakes escalate.** Each beat raises what's at stake or what's possible. Never a flat middle.
6. **Save the best for last.** In lists, the strongest item is the last one, and the hook or an early line says so ("number 5 is the one nobody uses").
7. **One throughline.** One video, one idea. No detour into a second topic, however good.

Re-hook lines that work (vary them, never the same twice in a batch): "But here's where it gets useful", "And that's not even the good part", "Now watch what happens when…", "The problem is…", a number that changes on screen, a sudden cut to the result.

## Beat maps (30-45s)

### Tool drop / paid vs free
| Beat | Time | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0-2s | Price or pain + the free alternative exists (don't name it yet or name it and withhold what it does) |
| Proof flash | 2-6s | Show the output right away. Pattern interrupt if possible ("that wasn't me") |
| Name + credibility | 6-12s | What it is, one proof (stars, who uses it, the price it replaces) |
| How it works | 12-25s | The mechanism in 2 steps the viewer can picture. Re-hook in the middle |
| Why it matters to *them* | 25-33s | Translate to the viewer's money/time. This is the escalation |
| CTA | last 3-5s | Keyword → the full setup |

### Counted list ("5 X that Y")
| Beat | Time | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0-2s | Count + outcome. Direct promise, no callout needed |
| Items 1..n-1 | 2-28s | One sentence each: name + what happens when you use it, with the result on screen. Increasing strength |
| Tease the last | somewhere mid | "The last one is the one nobody uses" |
| Best item | 28-35s | The strongest, with the clearest proof |
| CTA | last 3-5s | "Comment X for the other N" / the full list |

### Test / experiment
| Beat | Time | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0-2s | What I tried + the stake (money, time, a bet) |
| Setup | 2-8s | Rules of the test, why it might fail |
| Progress | 8-25s | 2-3 checkpoints with numbers. One setback (re-hook) |
| Result | 25-35s | The number. Honest, even if mixed |
| CTA | last 3-5s | The exact setup |

### Contrarian
| Beat | Time | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0-2s | Against what the niche repeats |
| Why they're wrong | 2-12s | The hidden cause (new information, not opinion) |
| Proof | 12-22s | Case, number or demo |
| What to do instead | 22-33s | The concrete alternative |
| CTA | last 3-5s | Resource or save |

### Demo / transformation
| Beat | Time | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0-2s | The after, or the pain of the before |
| Before | 2-7s | How bad it is today, on screen |
| The move | 7-25s | The steps, each with visible change. Re-hook at the midpoint |
| After | 25-33s | Side by side with the before |
| CTA | last 3-5s | The template/setup |

### Story
| Beat | Time | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0-2s | The moment of highest tension, first |
| Context | 2-10s | Just enough to understand the stakes |
| Turn | 10-25s | What went wrong / what changed. Pattern interrupt |
| Lesson as a tool | 25-35s | What the viewer can do with it tomorrow |
| CTA | last 3-5s | |

## Value, defined

Value is what the viewer can **see change** or **do tomorrow**. A tip read aloud is weak value. The same tip with the output on screen, or turned into a number the viewer cares about (hours, dollars, views), is strong value. When the creator's rules say to tease instead of giving everything away, show the input and the output and keep the full process for the CTA.
